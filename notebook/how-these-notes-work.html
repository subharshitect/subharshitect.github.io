How these notes work

17 October 2026observationmeta

This is a template, not a real note. Copy it to notebook/<slug>.html, add the same slug to data/notes.json, run python3 tools/build.py, and the note appears in the notebook and, if it is among the newest three, on the homepage.

Every note starts with a short introduction: what the question was and why it came up, in two or three sentences. Write it so someone who only reads this paragraph knows whether to keep going.1

1 Margin notes are for small pieces of context that would interrupt the paragraph. On a wide screen they sit beside it; on a phone they appear just below it. ↩

The main note

Then the substance: what was measured or tried, under what conditions, and what happened. Keep observations and interpretation apart. “Allocated memory rose by 0.035 MiB per step” is an observation; “that is the KV cache” is an interpretation, and it should say why.

Code goes in plain blocks, short enough to read:

python3 -m http.server 8000   # preview the site locally

What remains uncertain

Every note ends by saying what it does not show: the conditions it was not tested under, the explanation that is still a guess, the number that should be rechecked.
