Studies

The original

In 1943 Warren McCulloch, a neurophysiologist, and Walter Pitts, a self-taught logician, proposed that a neuron could be idealised as an all-or-none device. It fires when its excitation exceeds a fixed threshold, and an inhibitory input can stop it from firing. From that idealisation they showed that networks of such units realise expressions of logic, and that any such expression can be realised by some network. Loops in the network, they noted, let it hold on to the past.

Nearly every neural network built since descends from that paper. The units changed (real-valued weights, smooth activations, learning), but the idea did not: a mind-like behaviour assembled from many small comparisons.

Warren S. McCulloch and Walter Pitts, “A logical calculus of the ideas immanent in nervous activity”, Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics 5, 115–133 (1943). doi:10.1007/BF02478259

What the plate shows

Four layers of 6, 12, 10 and 6 units, connected by fixed random weights. The inputs on the left are slow waves. Each unit adds up its weighted inputs and a bias. A ReLU unit, the modern kind, passes on how far that total is above zero. An all-or-none unit, closer to the 1943 neuron, either fires or stays dark. Brighter nodes and lines carry more signal.

Lower the bias and watch whole paths go dark. With all-or-none units the network becomes a pattern of switches, and the same inputs light a smaller, harder-edged set of paths.

What it does not show

Nothing here learns: the weights are drawn once from the seed and never change. The 1943 model had no weights in this sense, only excitatory and inhibitory connections with an absolute veto, so the all-or-none setting is a resemblance, not a reconstruction. Each layer is rescaled so its brightest unit is fully lit, so compare units within a layer, not across layers. Real neurons are not like this.

Reflection

Nothing in a single unit knows anything. A unit is a comparison: more than this, or not. Whatever the network seems to know lives in the arrangement, in which comparisons feed which. In 1943 two people wrote down the possibility that thought could be made of such comparisons. We now build them by the billion and still argue about what, if anything, they know.
