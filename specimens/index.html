Specimens

Small systems from papers that changed how we think about pattern, computation and life.

Each plate reconstructs one idea from its original paper, runs it, and asks what it means. They open still; press Observe to watch one unfold. Read in order, they make a short lineage: from a single unit, to pattern, to unpredictability, to cooperation, to computation nobody designed, to chance.

  1. Plate I · 1943

    Feedforward

    What can a unit that only adds and compares come to know?

    McCulloch & Pitts, Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics

  2. Plate II · 1952 · 1993

    Gray–Scott

    How does something uniform become patterned?

    Turing, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B; Pearson, Science

  3. Plate III · 1983

    Rule 30

    Can a rule be simple and its future still unknowable?

    Wolfram, Reviews of Modern Physics

  4. Plate IV · 1992

    Kaleidoscopes of cooperation

    Can cooperation survive without anyone choosing to be good?

    Nowak & May, Nature

  5. Plate V · 1994

    Evolving computers

    Can computation appear without a designer?

    Das, Mitchell & Crutchfield, PPSN III

  6. Plate VI · 2007

    Genetic sectors

    How much of what survives is merit, and how much is position?

    Hallatschek, Hersen, Ramanathan & Nelson, PNAS

  7. Plate VII · 2026

    A machine examining itself

    What does a machine look like when it examines itself?

    a profiler trace of opt-125m, recorded on an H200
