Gray–Scott

How does something uniform become patterned?

Studies

The original

In 1952 Alan Turing asked how an embryo, which starts out nearly uniform, comes to have spots, stripes and limbs. His answer was chemical: substances he called morphogens, reacting with each other and diffusing through tissue, could make a uniform state unstable, so that small random disturbances grow into a stable pattern.

Forty years later John Pearson simulated a two-chemical reaction studied by Peter Gray and Stephen Scott, and found a surprising variety of irregular patterns in it, among them spots that grow until they reach a critical size and then divide in two.

A. M. Turing, “The chemical basis of morphogenesis”, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London B 237, 37–72 (1952). doi:10.1098/rstb.1952.0012
John E. Pearson, “Complex patterns in a simple system”, Science 261, 189–192 (1993). doi:10.1126/science.261.5118.189

What the plate shows

Chemical U is fed in everywhere. Chemical V turns U into more V and is slowly removed. Only V is drawn. Two numbers decide everything: the feed rate F and the kill rate k. The Mitosis study reproduces Pearson’s dividing spots; Coral, Maze and Worms are other regions of the same two-number map.

Nudge F or k by a single step and see whether the pattern survives, freezes, or fades back to nothing.

What it does not show

This is two equations on a small grid whose edges wrap around. Pearson found that these patterns need a finite disturbance to start, so the plate begins from a few seeded patches rather than from noise. The plate draws square cells, so shapes keep their proportions. Real tissues involve many more molecules, growth and mechanics.

Reflection

A perfectly uniform world has nothing to say. Pattern begins as a failure of uniformity: an instability that a small accident is enough to trigger, and that the rules then amplify into something that looks deliberate. Turing’s claim was not that pattern is designed, but that it is what certain kinds of sameness cannot help becoming.
